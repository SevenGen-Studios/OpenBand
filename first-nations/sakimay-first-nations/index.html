Brief History

Sources Verified

Sakimay First Nations’ written treaty history includes Chief Sakimay, whose name also appears as Sah-ka-ma or Sah-kee-may, signing Treaty 4 on September 15, 1874. The reserve formed part of the western Crooked Lake reserve district. After the chief’s death, Yellow Calf acted as a spokesman in dealings with the Indian Agent during the early 1880s. The community’s land base includes the Sakimay, Shesheep, Little Bone and Minoahcuk reserves. Agriculture and later tourism appear in the historical account. The treaty record and reserve names capture only part of the people’s continuing history. [1]
